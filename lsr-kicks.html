Overview

I started LSR Kicks with no outside funding and no team. As the only person running it, I was responsible for every decision: what to buy, how to price it, how to reach customers, and how to keep accurate financial records while studying full time.

Over nearly three years the business generated more than $100K in total sales across New Jersey and New York.

How the business ran

  1. Research. I tracked market trends and sales performance to decide which products were worth stocking.
  2. Listing. I listed inventory on an Instagram storefront that grew to more than 1,500 listings.
  3. Selling and service. I answered more than 1,000 customer inquiries myself and closed sales directly with buyers.
  4. Bookkeeping. I recorded every transaction, receivable, and expense so I always knew where the business stood.

Managing the finances

Because every sale and purchase ran through me, keeping clean records was the only way to know whether the business was actually making money. I tracked each transaction, followed up on money owed, and logged expenses so I could measure performance and plan purchases.

Growing the brand

I built the storefront's audience through regular, high-quality content, growing it to more than 1,500 followers. I studied engagement metrics and market research to refine what I posted and to spot which products were trending before buying.

What this shows

  • I can manage a business's financial records end to end, from transactions to receivables to expenses.
  • I make decisions with data, using sales metrics and market research to guide what to buy.
  • I can balance responsibility for real money with a full course load.
